# Personal CRM User Guide

This guide describes a practical daily workflow for using the CRM to answer
four questions:

1. What do I know about this prospect?
2. What happened previously?
3. What do I think?
4. What should I do next?

The CRM is deliberately organized around those questions. Keep factual
information, conversation history, personal opinions, and planned work in their
proper places.

## Start with the main navigation

The top navigation provides four working areas:

- **My Day** - the work that needs attention now.
- **Prospects** - search companies and find their opportunity workspace.
- **Opportunities** - search and filter the pipeline.
- **Follow-ups** - review actions by overdue, today, upcoming, or completed
  status.

The opportunity workspace is the main place to maintain a prospect's complete
record.

## 1. Add a new prospect

To add a completely new prospect:

1. Open **Companies** and select **Add Company**.
2. Enter the company name and any available factual details, then save.
3. Open **Contacts** and select **Add Contact**.
4. Select the company, enter the person's details, and save.
5. Open **Opportunities** and select **Add Opportunity**.
6. Select the company, enter the opportunity details, and save.
7. Open the company with **View** to confirm its contacts and opportunities.
8. Open the opportunity to add an assessment, next action, or future plan.

When creating the initial records, use one company per organization, attach
each person as a contact, and create a separate opportunity for each distinct
business possibility.

## 2. Record a conversation

Record the conversation as soon as practical while the facts are still clear.

1. Open the relevant opportunity from **Prospects**, **Opportunities**, or
   **My Day**.
2. In the **Opportunity** section, select **Add interaction**.
3. Enter the actual date and time of the call, meeting, email, or message.
4. Select the interaction type:
   - Call
   - Email
   - Meeting
   - Message
   - Demo
   - Note
   - Other
5. Give it a short, searchable subject.
6. Write the details as a factual summary:
   - What was discussed?
   - What information was shared?
   - What did the prospect ask for?
7. Record the outcome separately.
8. Select **Save**.

Write what happened, not what you think it means. For example:

> Prospect confirmed that the finance team will review pricing next week.

Avoid putting your interpretation in the interaction record. Put that in
**My assessment**.

The interaction appears in the chronological timeline under **What happened
previously?**

## 3. Write your assessment

After recording the facts, record your personal interpretation.

1. Open the opportunity.
2. Select **Add assessment**.
3. In **My assessment / thoughts**, write what you currently believe:
   - How strong is the opportunity?
   - How engaged is the prospect?
   - What evidence supports your view?
   - What is the likely buying situation?
4. Record any doubts in **Concerns**.
5. Select an **Opportunity level**:
   - Low
   - Medium
   - High
6. Select **Save**.

Be explicit and concise. A useful assessment explains the decision you are
making, not just a feeling:

> High opportunity. The problem is confirmed and the decision-maker is
> engaged, but budget approval is still uncertain.

Keep assessments separate from facts because your interpretation may change
even when the underlying conversation history does not.

## 4. Create the next action

The next action is the single concrete thing you intend to do next.

1. Open the opportunity.
2. Select **Add next action**.
3. Describe the action as a verb:
   - Send pricing options
   - Call the finance lead
   - Schedule a product demonstration
   - Ask for the procurement timeline
4. Set a due date whenever the action should happen on a specific day.
5. Choose a priority:
   - Low
   - Medium
   - High
6. Add useful context in **Notes**.
7. Select **Save**.

Prefer one clear next action over a paragraph of intentions. If there are
several independent tasks, create separate follow-ups so each can be scheduled
and completed.

## 5. Create a future plan

Use a future plan for an intended direction that is not the immediate next
task.

1. Open the opportunity.
2. Select **Add future plan**.
3. Describe the longer-term plan in **Future plan**.
4. Add a target date if one is known.
5. Select the appropriate status:
   - Planned
   - Active
   - Completed
   - Abandoned
6. Select **Save**.

Examples:

- Revisit expansion after the initial project is delivered.
- Introduce the operations team after the technical evaluation.
- Review renewal opportunity in the next quarter.

The distinction is:

- **Next action:** the next thing I will do.
- **Future plan:** what I intend to pursue later.

## 6. Use My Day

Open **My Day** at the start of the day. It is designed to answer:
**What should I work on today?**

Review the sections in this order:

### Overdue

These actions need attention because their due date has passed. Decide whether
to complete them, do them today, or create a more realistic replacement date.

### Today

These are the actions due today. Start here after resolving urgent overdue
items.

### Upcoming

Use this section to prepare for the next few days. Check that the planned
actions are still sensible and that high-priority work has enough preparation
time.

### Recent activity

Use recent interactions to refresh your memory before making calls or sending
messages.

### Important opportunities

These are high-priority active opportunities. Open them to review the company
facts, timeline, assessment, and next action before working on them.

Each action shows the company, opportunity, description, due date, and
priority. Select **Open opportunity** to see the complete context.

When an action is finished, select **Complete**. The CRM records the completion
date and time and removes it from the pending work while preserving its
history.

Use **Add follow-up** on My Day when you already know what needs to be done and
want to schedule it quickly. Select the opportunity, enter the action, choose
the due date and priority, and save.

## 7. Manage overdue follow-ups

Do not allow overdue actions to become a passive backlog.

1. Open **My Day** or **Follow-ups**.
2. Select the **Overdue** view.
3. For each item, open the opportunity and decide:
   - Complete it now if it was done but not recorded.
   - Work it today if it is still the correct action.
   - Create a replacement follow-up with a realistic due date if the plan
     changed.
4. Add an interaction after contacting the prospect.
5. Update your assessment if the situation has changed.
6. Complete the old action only when its actual work is finished.

Do not mark an action complete merely to make the overdue list look clean.
Preserving accurate history is more valuable than having zero overdue items.

## 8. Close an opportunity

When the opportunity reaches a final outcome, update its status from the
opportunity record or the opportunity management workflow:

- **Won** when the business has been secured.
- **Lost** when the opportunity will not proceed.

Before closing it:

1. Add the final interaction or decision as a factual timeline entry.
2. Record the reason or relevant context in the opportunity notes.
3. Complete remaining actions that were actually done.
4. Leave uncompleted actions alone if they represent work that did not happen;
   review them as part of cleanup.
5. Update the assessment if the final result changes your understanding.

Use **Paused** when the opportunity is not decided but active work has stopped.
Do not use **Lost** simply because there has been a delay.

## 9. Search for prospects

### Search by prospect details

1. Open **Prospects**.
2. Use **Search prospects**.
3. Search by:
   - Company name
   - Contact name
   - Phone number
   - Email address
4. Pause briefly after typing; the search is debounced and sent to the
   server.
5. Select **Open prospect** on the matching company.

The search and page are kept in the URL. If you open a detail page and select
**Back to list**, the search and page state are preserved.

### Search and filter opportunities

Open **Opportunities** to search by title or company and filter by:

- Status
- Priority
- Industry
- Source
- Expected decision date

Use **Follow-ups** for action-specific views: **Overdue**, **Today**,
**Upcoming**, and **Completed**.

## 10. Maintain good CRM data

### Keep facts factual

Put company identity, industry, source, location, phone numbers, email
addresses, and contact roles in the appropriate company or contact fields.
Avoid storing opinions in general notes.

### Use one clear record

Before adding a company, search first. Avoid duplicate companies with small
name variations. Use one contact record per person and one opportunity per
distinct business possibility.

### Write short, useful summaries

Use a consistent pattern for interactions:

> Date/context - what happened - important detail - outcome.

Write enough that you can understand the record weeks later without relying on
memory.

### Keep the next action current

Every active opportunity should have one obvious next action where possible.
Give it a date and priority. Replace stale actions rather than accumulating
vague reminders.

### Separate the four types of information

| Information | Where it belongs |
|---|---|
| Who the prospect is | Company and Contact |
| What happened | Interaction |
| What I think | Assessment |
| What I will do next | Action/Follow-up |
| What I intend to do later | Future Plan |

### Review stale records

At least once a week, review active opportunities with no recent interaction,
overdue actions, and opportunities with no next action. Update or close records
instead of leaving them ambiguous.

## Recommended 10-15 minute daily workflow

Use this routine at the beginning or end of each workday:

### Minute 0-2: Open My Day

Review **Overdue** and **Today**. Choose the two or three actions that matter
most today.

### Minute 2-5: Clear obvious overdue items

Complete actions that were already done. For genuinely outstanding work,
decide whether to do it today or schedule a realistic new follow-up.

### Minute 5-8: Prepare for the first important contact

Open the opportunity. Read the company information, contacts, recent
interactions, current assessment, and next action. This prevents asking for
information that is already known.

### Minute 8-11: Record recent activity

Add an interaction for any call, email, meeting, or message from the previous
day. Keep it factual and concise.

### Minute 11-13: Update your thinking

If the new information changes the situation, add an assessment or revise the
current assessment. Note concerns explicitly.

### Minute 13-15: Set up tomorrow

Create or adjust the next actions and future plans for the opportunities you
worked on. Confirm that each important active opportunity has a clear next
step and a sensible date.

The goal is not to fill every field. The goal is to leave the CRM accurate
enough that the next work session starts with context, not reconstruction.
